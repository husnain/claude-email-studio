# The Steep Club — Launch Campaign Plan

**Date:** 2026-11-09
**Campaign window:** 4 weeks starting Monday, 2026-12-07
**Goal:** 300 waitlist members start a subscription by end of week 4

---

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** The Steep Club Is Open

**Summary:** A four-week email sequence turning 2,000 waitlist members into paying subscribers, using segmented messaging to meet each group where they are.

**Primary objective:** 300 waitlist conversions to paid subscription in the first month (15% conversion rate against the 2,000-person list).

**Secondary objectives:**
- Re-engage cold or inactive waitlist members before they forget they signed up
- Establish the brand tone — simple, warm, knowledgeable — in the first impression
- Surface and understand why non-converters didn't join (exit survey or unsubscribe reason)

---

## 2. Target Audience

Three segments, one list. Each gets messaging matched to their reason for signing up.

### Segment A — Tea-Curious Beginners (~35% of list, ~700 people)
They are interested but uncertain. They don't know where to start, worry about buying the wrong thing, and wonder if a subscription is worth it before they know what they like. The job of these emails is to make starting feel easy and low-risk.

### Segment B — Gift Buyers (~25% of list, ~500 people)
They joined thinking about someone else. They want to look thoughtful without doing a lot of research. The job of these emails is to make gifting The Steep Club feel like the obvious right move — and to make the case that it's a gift the giver will want to keep.

### Segment C — Daily Tea Drinkers (~40% of list, ~800 people)
They already drink tea every day. They are looking for variety and quality they can't find at a supermarket. The job of these emails is to show that The Steep Club sources and curates better than they can on their own.

**Channels where this audience lives:** Email (what they signed up for). No paid ads needed — this is a warm list.

**Buying stage:** Consideration to decision. They raised their hand; now we need to give them a reason to act.

---

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message:** The Steep Club is open. Here's what you get when you join today.

### By segment:

**Beginners**
- "You don't need to know anything about tea to love what lands in your post."
- "Every box comes with tasting notes written for people who don't speak tea."
- "If you don't like something, tell us. We'll fix the next box."
- Proof point: Onboarding quiz takes 90 seconds and shapes the first box.

**Gift buyers**
- "It's a gift they'll think about every morning."
- "Ships directly to them. Gift note included."
- "No awkward expiry dates. Cancel or pause any time."
- Proof point: Gifting flow is a separate checkout — no account needed for the recipient.

**Daily drinkers**
- "These teas aren't in your supermarket. That's the point."
- "Every tea is sourced directly from the farm or collective."
- "Tell us what you drank last month and we won't repeat it."
- Proof point: Sourcing transparency — region, harvest, producer name on every bag.

---

## 4. Channel Strategy

**Email only.** The list is warm and email is what they opted into — this is the highest-leverage channel available.

| Channel | Why it fits | Format | Effort |
|---------|-------------|--------|--------|
| Email (segmented) | Direct line to people who already said yes | Plain-text with one image per email | Medium |
| Email (broadcast) | Launch and urgency moments reach everyone | Same format | Low |

No blog, no social, no paid ads for this campaign. We can repurpose emails to other channels after the launch window, but that is out of scope here.

## 7. Success Metrics

**Primary KPI:** 300 new subscribers from the waitlist by 2026-12-31

**How to track:** Subscriber count at campaign start vs. end, filtered to source = waitlist.

| Metric | Target | How to track |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| Subscriptions from waitlist | 300 (15% of 2,000) | Subscription platform, source tag |
| Email open rate | ≥ 40% (warm list benchmark) | Email platform |
| Click-through rate | ≥ 5% per send | Email platform |
| Unsubscribe rate | < 1% per send | Email platform (flag if above) |
| Conversion by segment | Track A, B, C separately | Segment tags in email platform |
| Re-engagement rate (week 3) | ≥ 15% open among non-openers | Email platform segment |

**Reporting cadence:** Check open and click rates 24 hours after each send. Review conversion count at the end of each week. Full campaign review at the end of week 4.

## 8. Budget

Email-only. No paid ads.

Likely costs are limited to:
- Email platform send costs (depends on current plan and list size — 2,000 contacts, ~11 sends over 4 weeks)
- Any launch offer discount or free-first-box incentive (optional; decide before drafting)
- Copywriting time (internal)

No design budget assumed — plain-text emails perform well for this tone and audience. Add a single header image per email if design capacity allows, but do not make it a dependency.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Waitlist has gone cold — low open rates | Medium | Teaser email (2026-12-04) acts as a re-warm. If open rate on teaser is under 20%, delay launch by one week and send a second warm-up. |
| Segment tags are wrong or missing | Medium | Audit segment data before 2026-12-04. If clean segmentation isn't possible, run a one-question preference email ("Which sounds like you?") before the launch week. |
| 15% conversion target is too ambitious | Medium | The 15% target is achievable for a warm, opted-in list with a clear offer. If week-1 conversion is under 5%, pull the objection email forward to week 2 and test a launch offer (discount or bonus). |
| Gift buyers need earlier urgency | Low-Medium | Christmas delivery window is tight. If UK/international shipping, the gift deadline email (2026-12-23) may need to move to 2026-12-18. Confirm fulfilment timelines before scheduling. |
